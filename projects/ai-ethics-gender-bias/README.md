# The Ethical Implications of the Over-Sexualisation of Women in Artificial Intelligence

## Overview

This 2,937-word ethics essay examines the over-sexualisation of women in AI-generated imagery and connects that issue to broader concerns about dataset bias, platform incentives, algorithmic discrimination and unequal representation in AI development.

It is a critical, literature-based writing sample rather than an empirical or coding project.

## Central argument

The essay argues that AI systems can reproduce and amplify patterns of discrimination present in their training data and social environments. It challenges the idea that inspection of a model's technical internals is sufficient to solve algorithmic sexism, and emphasises representative data, institutional incentives and the social context in which automated systems are deployed.

## Structure and themes

1. **How AI systems operate:** training data and design/optimisation choices can shape what models generate or prioritise.
2. **Identifying algorithmic bias:** the essay discusses how biased datasets and proxy variables can encode gender stereotypes.
3. **Consequences:** it considers career opportunity, mental health, privacy, deepfake abuse and unequal error rates.
4. **Algorithmic affirmative action:** it discusses representative datasets, reweighting, adversarial debiasing, fairness constraints and greater participation of women in AI research and development.
5. **Conclusion:** it argues that technical systems are not socially neutral and that meaningful fairness requires institutional and societal change as well as technical interventions.

## Relevance to AI research

The essay supports an interdisciplinary interest in responsible AI, dataset documentation, fairness evaluation and the social consequences of generative systems. It complements technical projects by showing awareness that model performance and deployment impacts need to be evaluated in their social context.

## Scope and limitations

- The paper is a literature-based argument and does not present a new empirical dataset, model or fairness experiment.
- Claims and examples are drawn from the literature cited in the essay; they should not be presented as original empirical findings by the author.
- The original coursework PDF contains third-party illustrative images. This README summarises the argument without republishing those images.
